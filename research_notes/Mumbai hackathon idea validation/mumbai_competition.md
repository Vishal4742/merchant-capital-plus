# Competitive landscape at ETHGlobal Mumbai (Nov 5–7 2026) and the Whitechain ecosystem

Research done 2026-11-05 (IST). Explorer numbers were fetched live from the Whitechain Sepolia Blockscout API that day and will drift. Every estimate is labelled as an estimate, with its basis.

## 1. How many hackers and teams to expect, and what comparable ETHGlobal events drew

### Takeaway
ETHGlobal has not published an expected headcount for Mumbai. Its two closest precedents are New Delhi 2025 (616 projects, India) and Bangkok 2024 (713 projects, Devcon week). 2026 in-person events have been smaller, at about 230–255 projects each. My estimate for Mumbai is 450–700 projects from roughly 1,000–1,700 hackers, but treat it as an estimate, not a reported figure.

### Cited Findings
- None of the Mumbai pages (event, prizes, info/start) gives an expected number of hackers or projects. The info/start page caps teams at 5, allows solo hacking, requires every member to be accepted and to stake ETH, and sets a hard deadline of Sat Nov 7, 09:00 IST with no late submissions. Teams form via the Hacker Dashboard, Discord #find-a-team, and organizer-run team formation calls — [ETHGlobal Mumbai info/start](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/start)
- The top 10 teams become Finalists. The Finalist prize is 1,000 USDC + 500 travel per person for teams of 3–5, and 250 USDC per person for solo or 2-person teams (a 2-person team also gets 250 travel each) — [ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)
- Hacking was moved one day earlier, to Thu–Sat Nov 5–7, during Devcon India — [ETHGlobal on X](https://x.com/ETHGlobal/status/2099941061812703243). Devcon 8 itself runs Nov 3–6 in Mumbai — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/devcon-8-mumbai-november-2026/), [EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/12/23/devcon-mumbai)
- ETHGlobal offered a Hacker Pack that discounts or waives Devcon 8 tickets ($99 single-day pass for Day 1 or 2, Days 3–4 free, limited quantity), plus a free Pragma Network Pass for confirmed hackers. This is an extra pull for out-of-town hackers — [ETHGlobal X account](https://x.com/ETHGlobal) (via search snippet), [hashtagweb3 listing](https://hashtagweb3.com/ethglobal-mumbai)
- **Tokyo 2026 (Sep 25–27):** 504 hackers, 254 projects, "next stop: Mumbai" — [ETHGlobal on X](https://x.com/ETHGlobal/status/2104153317257740554)
- **New York 2026 (Jun 12–14):** 682 participants from 44 countries, 486 developers, 232 projects — [ESILV article, Jun 26 2026](https://www.esilv.fr/ethglobal-new-york-les-equipes-de-devinci-blockchain-remportent-cinq-recompenses-internationales/). Top-10 post: [ETHGlobal on X](https://x.com/ETHGlobal/status/2066247824967340122)
- **Cannes 2026 (Apr 3–5):** 10 finalists (ENShell, DIVE, maki, Défi, ALMA, npmguard, VEIL VPN, PaintGlobal, EVM PORST, Corpus). No project count was published in the articles I found — [crypto.news, Apr 6 2026](https://crypto.news/ai-agents-privacy-and-prediction-markets-define-ethglobal-cannes-2026-finalists/), [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/019d60d1-6c8f-77b9-95a0-23c8ccdde03a)

### Inferences
- **Estimate: 450–700 Mumbai projects, central guess about 550.** Basis: the two precedents that match Mumbai best (India: 616; Devcon week: 713) sit at 600–700. I discounted that because every 2026 in-person event I could count landed at 232–254 projects, and New York fell from 275 in 2025 to 232 in 2026 (−16%). I can't separate the 2026 decline from city effects, so the range is wide.
- **Estimate: about 1,000–1,700 hackers.** Basis: hacker-to-project ratios of 2.1 (NY 2026: 486/232), 2.0 (Tokyo 2026: 504/254) and about 2.6 (New Delhi 2025, if the 1,600 figure holds), applied to the project range.
- **Estimate: about 1,100–1,750 sponsor-prize entries in total, roughly 90–145 per sponsor on average.** Basis: each project may enter at most 3 partner prizes ([prizes page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). I assumed an average of 2.5 picks per project across 12 sponsors. The 2.5 is my assumption, not a published number.
- With 12 sponsors instead of New Delhi's 19, each sponsor should get more entries per prize dollar than at New Delhi.
- Mumbai's Finalist prize is worth far more per person to a 3–5 person team than to a solo or 2-person team. That pushes strong hackers toward larger teams, which in turn means fewer but stronger rival projects.

### Gaps
- No official or announced acceptance count for Mumbai. ETHGlobal's wrap-up post, which normally gives "N hackers, M projects", will only appear after Nov 7.
- I found no project counts for Cannes 2026 or Lisbon 2026.
- Per-sponsor entry counts are not published for any past ETHGlobal event, so every per-track number below is a model, not data.

## 2. What hackers are saying they'll build, and which tracks look crowded or empty

### Takeaway
Public "here's what I'm building at Mumbai" chatter is close to zero. Searches of X, Reddit and GitHub turned up no team-formation threads and only two public repos aimed at Mumbai. Both are Continuity projects that stack World ID + ENS + Hedera/0G/Arc x402. The tracks most likely to be crowded are Uniswap, ENSv2, World and Hedera x402. EF Frame-Tx, Arkiv node tooling, 0G Physical AI and the three non-Continuity Whitechain tracks look thin. The Whitechain testnet already shows at least four payment or RWA builds deployed before hacking began (see Q3).

## 3. Whitechain: existing dApps and tooling, community, what the team wants, and who built DeliveryCheckout

### Takeaway
Whitechain is a young OP-Stack L2. It relaunched Aug 18 2026, has a testnet (1874) with only 64 USDT holders and 380 verified contracts, and its mainnet status is murky across its own pages. There is no organic community discussion. The sponsor wants merchant-grade USDT flows with onchain enforcement, explicitly not plain transfers. Since Oct 16, at least four teams have deployed payment or RWA contracts that look aimed at these tracks. One of them is the "OpenBox" DeliveryCheckout, an escrow checkout that also wires in World ID couriers and a Hedera HCS audit log. I could not identify its builder.

**What the sponsor wants** — all from [ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)
- Rules for every track: contracts verified on Whitechain Sepolia (1874); at least one USDT payment (USDT `0xcedfB56113143d55b2358b8CFbB0dEB5Ad2f55a7`) in the main flow, with an explorer link; a README explaining why onchain stablecoin settlement is needed; steady commits; a 2–4 min demo showing each party; and WHITECHAIN_FEEDBACK.md.
- **Operate** wants checkout widgets, payment links/invoices, permit-pull subscriptions and refunds, with extra credit for flows beyond the first payment. Won't place: "A pay button that sends a plain transfer", tip jars, bill splitters, custodial bridges, or payroll/escrow/splits standing alone.
- **Tokenize** wants bonds with allowlists, invoice financing, repo with DvP and multi-party trade finance, with rules enforced in the contract. Invoice *payment* belongs in Operate and invoice *financing* in Tokenize.
- **Scale** wants gasless flows via permit or EIP-3009 plus a relayer, embedded smart accounts, and x402 "through a facilitator you run". Judges ask who pays gas and what stops relayer WBT drain.
- Workshop: "How to Win on Whitechain, Tracks and Tips", Thu Nov 5, 4:30 PM IST, Room 2, plus a recorded video. Hackers get free Ankr RPC (1,500 rps for 7 days). The prize page pitches Whitechain as an "onchain business layer" presented "with Optimism".

  - buyers can cancel before dispatch
  - dispatch binds a World-verified courier
  - handover is recorded with a seller-signed label
  - settle applies an inspector's signed verdict (pay courier and seller, or refund)
  - timeoutRelease pays out after the inspection window
  - lockPayout lets an allowlisted financier buy the seller payout
  - a refund pool handles serial-matched returns
